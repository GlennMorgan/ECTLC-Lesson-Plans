Vision: Seeing Where You Are Going

Duration: 30 minutes
Purpose: Help participants identify direction, not perfection

---

Opening and Framing

0–5 minutes

“When people hear the word vision, they think it means having everything figured out. That is not what vision is.

Vision is simply being able to see where you are going, even if the picture is incomplete.

If you do not know where you are going, every road feels pointless, and every setback feels personal.”

Ask the group:
  • “Have you ever worked hard at something and later realized it did not actually take you where you wanted to go?”
Key Point: Goals are things you do. Vision is who you are becoming and what kind of life you are moving toward.
---

Vision vs Goals: What’s the Difference?

5 minutes

“Most people are taught to set goals, but very few people are taught to develop vision.

When you have goals without vision, you can work very hard and still feel lost.

When you have vision without goals, you can feel inspired but stuck.”

What Vision Is

  • Vision is the picture of the life you want to move toward.
  • Vision answers the question: Who am I becoming?
  • Vision is long-term and direction-setting.
  • Vision does not need to be detailed to be useful.

What Goals Are

  • Goals are specific actions you take.
  • Goals answer the question: What am I doing next?
  • Goals are short-term and change often.
  • Goals can be adjusted without losing direction.
Vision is the destination. Goals are the steps that move you in that direction.

How Vision and Goals Work Together

  • Vision gives meaning to goals.
  • Goals give structure to vision.
  • If a goal fails, vision keeps you from quitting.
  • If vision feels distant, goals keep you moving.
Ask the group:
  • “Have you ever hit a goal and still felt empty?”
  • “Have you ever quit a goal because you didn’t know why it mattered?”

Why Vision Matters

5–10 minutes

“Imagine driving at night with no headlights. You might still move forward, but you are tense, reactive, and one surprise can wreck you.

Vision is headlights. It does not show you the whole journey, it just shows you enough to keep going.”

Core Teaching Points

  • Without vision, people drift, quit easily, or chase things that do not satisfy.
  • Without vision, pain feels pointless.
  • With vision, struggle has meaning.
  • With vision, choices become clearer.
---

Exercise: Mining for Vision Nuggets

10–20 minutes

Step 1: Set the Rules

  • No right or wrong answers.
  • Do not try to sound smart.
  • Do not edit yourself.
  • Write whatever comes up.

Step 2: Write One Question

Have participants write these three questions, then choose ONE:

  • What do I care deeply about?
  • What do I deeply desire?
  • What do I really want?

Instructions:

  • Set a 7 minute timer.
  • Write nonstop.
  • If stuck, write “I don’t know” until something comes.

Step 3: Circle the Gold

  • Read what you wrote.
  • Circle ONE sentence or phrase that feels real or important.
---

Pair Share: One Why Only

20–27 minutes
  • Pair up.
  • Partner A reads their circled sentence.
  • Partner B asks: “Why does that matter to you?”
  • Partner A answers once.
  • Switch roles.
This is listening, not fixing. One question is enough.
---

Daily Practice: Affirmations and Vision Nuggets

27–29 minutes

5 Affirmations (Fill Your Cup)

  • I am capable of growth.
  • I am learning to make better choices.
  • I am worthy of a meaningful life.
  • I can face today with courage.
  • I have something to offer others.

5 Vision Nuggets (Direction)

  • Who am I becoming?
  • What kind of person do I want to be known as?
  • What kind of relationships do I want?
  • What kind of peace do I want in my life?
  • What kind of legacy do I want to leave?
Affirmations build confidence. Vision gives direction. Together, they create movement.
---

Closing Reflection

29–30 minutes
Silent reflection:
  • “If I honored one vision nugget this week, what small decision might change?”

“You do not need to know the whole path. You only need to know what direction you are walking.”

Biblical Foundations for Vision

  • Proverbs 29:18 (ESV): “Where there is no vision, the people perish.”
  • Habakkuk 2:2–3 (ESV): “Write the vision; make it plain… For the vision awaits its appointed time.”
  • Proverbs 4:25–27 (ESV): “Let your eyes look directly forward, and your gaze be straight before you.”
  • Philippians 1:6 (ESV): “He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion.”
  • 2 Corinthians 4:18 (ESV): “We look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen.”
