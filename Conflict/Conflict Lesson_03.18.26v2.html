Reframing conflict
Core Idea
Critical truth
Conflict is not just about what happened.
It is about the meaning you assign to what happened.
The event matters, but the story you attach to the event often drives the emotional explosion.
Event + meaning + emotion + reaction
3
The conflict formula
How it starts

The formula behind most conflict

1
EventSomeone says something, does something, forgets something, gives correction, or uses a tone.
2
StoryYou interpret it. “They disrespected me.” “They think I am stupid.”
3
EmotionAnger, shame, hurt, fear, defensiveness, or pride rises.
4
ReactionTone changes, body tightens, voice rises, sarcasm comes out, shutdown begins.
5
EscalationThe other person reacts to your reaction, and now the real problem gets buried.

Most conflict is no longer about the original issue. It becomes reactions to reactions.

Interrupt the cycle early
4
Three traps
Where we get lost

The 3 traps that pour gasoline on conflict

Trap 1

Mind Reading

Assuming intent without evidence.

“They did that on purpose.”

Trap 2

Personalizing

Making everything about your value, your respect, your identity.

“This is about me.”

Trap 3

Story Building

Adding a whole narrative to a moment.

“They always do this. Nobody respects me.”

Assumption drives escalation

What is underneath anger
Secondary emotion

Anger is often not the root emotion

What shows up
  • Raised voice
  • Sharp tone
  • Defensiveness
  • Threats, sarcasm, shutdown
↓
What may be underneath
  • Hurt
  • Fear
  • Embarrassment
  • Rejection
  • Shame
  • Feeling unheard or forgotten

Mature conflict work asks: “What am I actually feeling under this anger?”

Address the real issue, not just the loud emotion

The pause
Your power point
This is the superpower
Between trigger and response, there is a space.
In that space lies your freedom.
When you lose the pause, your body takes over. When you find the pause, your character can lead.
Do not confuse impulse with wisdom
7
The 4 P's
Practical Tool

The 4 P's for Conflict

Pause

Do not react instantly. Slow the moment down before your emotions take over.

Be Present

Do an inventory. What is happening in your mind and body right now?

Be Patient

Give yourself and the other person time. Clarity comes after intensity drops.

Pray

Invite God into the moment. Ask for wisdom, humility, and the right response.

Flow: Pause → Present → Patient → Pray → Then Respond

This is how you slow conflict down
8
Conflict reset
5 step framework

The Conflict Reset Framework

1
PauseDo not answer instantly. Give your nervous system time to settle.
2
Check the storyAsk, “What am I telling myself right now?”
3
Fact vs storySeparate what actually happened from what you believe it means.
4
Name the real emotionIs this anger, or is it hurt, fear, shame, or pride?
5
Choose responseAsk, “What response will I respect tomorrow?”
Skill beats impulse
9
Bad response vs strong response
Language matters

Same conflict, different outcomes

Escalates
  • “You always do this.”
  • “You are disrespecting me.”
  • “Whatever, I am done.”
  • Interrupting, mocking, threatening
  • Talking to win instead of understand
De-escalates
  • “Help me understand what you meant.”
  • “When that happened, I felt frustrated.”
  • “I need a minute so I can respond well.”
  • Ask one clarifying question
  • Address the issue, not the person
Tone is part of the message
