Conflict Class

ECTLC Life Skills Session

Purpose of This Class

Conflict is not the problem. Unresolved conflict is the problem. This class teaches how conflict starts, how it escalates, and how to resolve it in a way that protects growth, relationships, and personal stability.

Key Conflict Vocabulary

  • Conflict – tension between people caused by unmet expectations, hurt feelings, or misunderstanding.
  • Trigger – something that activates an emotional reaction connected to past wounds.
  • Assumption – believing something without having the full facts.
  • Escalation – when emotions increase and listening decreases.
  • Resolution – restoring peace, clarity, or workable boundaries.
  • Reconciliation – restoring trust after change and accountability.
  • Restitution – repairing damage caused by actions.
  • Accountability – taking responsibility without excuses or blame shifting.

The 4 Types of Conflict

  • Person vs Person – misunderstanding, disrespect, tone, expectations.
  • Person vs Self – guilt, shame, fear, indecision.
  • Person vs System – rules, structure, authority.
  • Person vs Circumstances – stress, hardship, limitations.
Most conflict in recovery environments is Person vs Person or Person vs Self.

What is Gossip (Biblical Definition)

Gossip is sharing information about someone that harms their character, spreads division, or reveals something that is not yours to share.

Why People Gossip

  • To feel powerful
  • To feel included
  • To avoid direct conversations
  • To seek validation
  • To avoid dealing with their own issues
  • Fear of confrontation
“Gossip is talking about someone in a way you would never talk to them.”

Scripture:

  • Proverbs 16:28 – A gossip separates close friends.
  • Proverbs 20:19 – A gossip betrays confidence.
  • Ephesians 4:29 – Let no corrupt talk come out of your mouth.

What Forgiveness Is (and Is Not)

Biblical forgiveness means releasing anger, bitterness, and revenge toward someone who hurt you.

Forgiveness Is Not

  • Approving behavior
  • Forgetting what happened
  • Allowing repeat harm
  • Immediate trust
  • Ignoring accountability

Forgiveness Is

  • A decision
  • A discipline
  • Emotional freedom
  • Letting go of resentment

Important Distinction:

  • Forgiveness restores your heart
  • Reconciliation restores the relationship

Forgiveness is unconditional. Reconciliation requires change.

The Repeat-Back Technique

Before responding, briefly repeat the other person’s main point.

Example Scripts

  • “What I hear you saying is ____. Did I get that right?”
  • “So you felt disrespected because ____. Is that accurate?”
  • “Before I respond, I want to make sure I understand you.”
People calm down when they feel understood.

The 4 Tactical Skills of Active Listening

  • Attention – stop multitasking, make eye contact.
  • Clarification – ask questions.
  • Reflection – repeat the main point.
  • Response – share your perspective calmly.

Acceptable Outcomes of Conflict Resolution

  • Mutual understanding
  • Behavior change agreement
  • Boundary agreement
  • Third-party mediation
  • Forgiveness without reconciliation

Resolution does not always mean restored friendship.

The Truth About Tone and Nonverbal Communication

You may have heard that 93% of communication is nonverbal. That is not accurate in general conversation. However, during emotional conflict situations, tone and body language strongly affect interpretation.

You can say the right words with the wrong tone and create a fight.

Let Your Yes Be Yes (Boundary Integrity)

“Let what you say be simply ‘Yes’ or ‘No’; anything more than this comes from evil.”
Matthew 5:37 (ESV)

Many conflicts happen because people say “no,” then talk themselves out of their own decision. They explain too much, soften boundaries, or try to avoid disappointing others.

What This Verse Teaches

  • Say what you mean
  • Do not negotiate your convictions
  • Do not soften truth to avoid discomfort
  • Clarity prevents conflict
  • Ambiguity creates conflict

Examples

  • “No, I’m not lending that.”
  • “No, I can’t be part of that conversation.”
  • “No, that crosses a boundary for me.”
Strong boundaries protect relationships. Weak boundaries create resentment.

Closing Principle

Conflict handled correctly produces growth. Conflict avoided produces distance. Conflict handled poorly produces damage.

Learning to resolve conflict is part of learning to live differently.
