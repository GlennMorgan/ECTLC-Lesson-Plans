ECTLC Recovery Life Lessons

Lesson Plan: When the Structure Is Gone, Staying Connected to God and Community

Core thesis: Sobriety is not sustained by environment control. It is sustained by connection, confession, and immediate intervention.

Audience: ECTLC Adult participants (men and families)
Time: 30 minutes
Materials: Whiteboard, pens, printed handout

Learning Objectives

  • Understand why isolation often comes before relapse.
  • Identify early relapse behaviors before substance use begins.
  • Create a “First 24 Hour Response Plan.”
  • Build a post-ECTLC connection strategy for Stability that is centered on God and community.
Key statement to repeat:
If you isolate, you deteriorate.

Section 1: The Myth of Strength

Teaching Point

Relapse rarely starts with a substance. It starts with disconnection.

Board Write

  • Emotional relapse
  • Mental relapse
  • Physical relapse

Discussion Questions

  • What changes first before someone uses?
  • What does isolation look like at ECTLC?
  • What does it look like outside of ECTLC?

Scripture Anchors

  • James 5:16, confession and prayer with others
  • Ecclesiastes 4:9-12, strength in partnership

Section 2: Why You Cannot Isolate at ECTLC and Why That Is Protection not Oppression

Many people experience structure as pressure. Reframe it as protection.

ECTLC Structure Provides

  • Accountability
  • Visibility, people can notice changes
  • Immediate intervention, support is nearby
  • Forced fellowship, you are around others
  • Correction and coaching in real time

Group Exercise

Prompt 1: What protects you here that will not exist outside?

Examples: accountability, curfew, drug tests, mandatory classes, staff oversight, work therapy, restricted environments and time management.

Prompt 2: What replaces those protections when you leave?

Let silence work. The goal is honest realism, not fear.

Section 3: Early Relapse Behaviors

Important: Do not wait until you are in “physical relapse” to act. By then you are already losing the fight.

Emotional Relapse (Common Indicators)

  • Irritability, anger, resentments
  • Skipping prayer, avoiding Scripture, spiritual numbness
  • Avoiding meetings, avoiding trusted people
  • Complaining and blaming, “nothing works here”
  • Overconfidence, “I got this”

Mental Relapse (Common Indicators)

  • Bargaining, “just once” thinking
  • Romanticizing the past
  • Secret planning, covering tracks
  • Minimizing consequences
  • Reconnecting with old people, places, patterns
  • Keep it simple, those within ECTLC that keep it simple, succeed. Don't make this simple progrqam complex. Those within ECTLC that make it complex, struggle.

Participant Exercise: My Top 3 Warning Signs

Write your personal early indicators. These are usually behaviors, moods, or choices, not “I used.”

Section 4: The First 24 Hour Response Plan

Principle: Delay increases danger. Secrecy accelerates relapse. Movement interrupts obsession.
Step Action
1. Call within 10 minutes I call: _______________________ (name) at _______________________ (phone)
2. Tell the truth completely No minimizing. No hiding. “Here is exactly what is happening.”
3. Physically relocate I move to a safe place: _______________________
4. Pray immediately Simple surrender prayer: “God, I cannot do this alone. Help me right now.”
5. Remove access I remove myself from triggers, money, transportation, contacts, or locations as needed.
6. Same-day meeting I attend a meeting, group, or church support the same day.

Mini Script (What to Say on the Phone)

This helps when you feel ashamed and cannot find words.

“I need help right now. I am having relapse thoughts/behaviors. I am not safe alone. Can you talk with me and help me take the next right step?”

Section 5: What Happens When You Leave

When structure drops, exposure rises. Recovery does not fail because you got weaker. It fails because protections were not replaced.

ECTLC Protection Replacement After Exit
Mandatory spiritual inputs Consistent church involvement, daily prayer and Scripture plan
Built-in accountability Sponsor/mentor and a weekly check-in schedule
Limited access to old patterns Boundaries, blocked contacts, avoided locations, new routines
Community around you Intentional fellowship, group attendance, service team
Work therapy and structured time Purposeful employment, service commitments, schedule discipline
